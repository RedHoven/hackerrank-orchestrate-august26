What each data family contributes

Incoming + labels

messages.csv is the unlabeled target. sample_messages.csv has the same inputs plus ground truth and is only for quality checks.

110 target rows: 63 group, 30 business, 17 personal. Media: 87 text, 15 image, 8 voice.

History + feedback

Historical messages become retrieval evidence; their event rows show whether the user valued, dismissed, muted, or reported similar content.

278 opens · 153 replies · 134 dismissals · 134 mutes · 55 reports.

Recipient preferences

User-level behavior and daily load calibrate interruption tolerance. Group memberships and business relationships provide the precise personalization layer.

A muted group changes a casual message; a business opt-out changes a promotion.

Sender legitimacy

Business account metadata distinguishes verified, matching-domain brands from potentially risky accounts. Personal sender trust is learned from past interactions.

Relevant checks: verified flag, official vs sender domain, account age, report volume.

Conversation setting

Groups encode social context—family, school, work, society, marketplace—and membership exposes roles, activity, notification dismissals, and mute state.

Use direct mentions and time-sensitive operations to override normal group muting.

Multimodal content

Image and voice catalogs map media IDs to assets. Their extracted text or transcript should be treated as additional message content, not a separate prediction target.

The target media mix is modest but high-value: 23 of 110 messages.

Decision path

Safety → relevance → urgency → interruption cost

First mute clear scams, spam, unwanted promotions, and low-value forwards. Then retrieve comparable history and adjust by sender trust, relationship status, group mute state, direct mention, time sensitivity, and media content. Finally choose notify only when the immediate benefit outweighs the user’s interruption cost; otherwise digest.
